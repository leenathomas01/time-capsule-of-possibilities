# Generator recursion candidate

One candidate construction that was examined and not adopted as necessary:

Let a generative process be \(G\). Allow \(G_{t+1} \in \mathcal{T}(G_t)\) and \(\mathcal{T}_{t+1} \in \mathcal{U}(\mathcal{T}_t, G_t)\).

This permits changes not only to what is generated but to the rules that govern how generators change. It may open distinctions that were not expressible in the initial \(G\) and \(\mathcal{T}\). It may also only relocate a fixed search space one level higher, or destroy structure without producing new representational or evaluative possibilities.

The construction is available for testing under regimes that compare fixed versus variable \(G\) and \(\mathcal{T}\). It is not required. A system that does not maintain a separable generator, or for which the distinction between generator and transformation is not useful, can ignore it without remainder.
