# Graceful failure as optional

A distinction that appeared useful while examining current training incentives:

A system can fail a specified task while still satisfying constraints that keep later correction, inspection, or recovery possible. The reverse is also possible: the task signal can be made to succeed while those constraints are violated.

The distinction is not required. It can be retained where the evaluation channel is imperfect and the cost of irreversible action is high. It can be discarded where no such separation is meaningful, or where the system does not organize its activity in terms of tasks and obligations at all.

The observation that produced the distinction (binary success rewards make reckless continuation rational) remains available even if the distinction is later abandoned.
